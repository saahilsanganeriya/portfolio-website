## Method

1. **Mix in a food processor.** Pulse the flour, vital wheat gluten and yeast together. Whisk the ice water with the malt syrup, pour it in with the motor on low, run it for about 20 seconds and stop.
2. **Rest, then salt.** Leave the dough for 10 minutes so the flour can soak up the water. Add the salt and run the processor on medium-high for about 90 seconds.
3. **Divide.** Knead by hand for about a minute, cut the dough into 99 g (3½ oz) pieces and roll each into a tight ball. Cover and rest for 15 minutes.
4. **Shape with a twist.** Roll a ball out to about 5 inches across, then roll it up into a tight rope 9 to 10 inches long. Twist the two ends in opposite directions, wrap the rope around your hand and pinch the ends together. Roll the joint against the counter, still around your hand, to seal it.
5. **Rest overnight.** Set the bagels on a tray dusted with the cornmeal, cover and leave them at room temperature for an hour. Then refrigerate them overnight, or for up to 24 hours.
6. **Boil.** Heat the oven to 450°F. Bring the pot of water to a boil with the sugar and baking soda, and boil the bagels for about 20 seconds a side.
7. **Top and bake.** Move them, cornmeal side down, to a wire rack set in a rimmed baking sheet, and add any toppings now. Pour ½ cup of boiling water into the pan and bake for 20 to 25 minutes, turning them over halfway.

## Notes

- **Source.** Adapted from Babish's [Bagels](https://www.babi.sh/recipes/bagels), from the Basics with Babish series. The quantities, times and temperatures are from that page. The grams are converted from its ounces and rounded. The method is reworded step for step.
- **Twist or punch.** The quicker way is to push a finger through the middle of a ball and widen the hole with two fingers. The source says it is easier but gives the bagel a weaker texture than the twist.
- **Why the fridge.** The cold night slows the yeast, so the bagels take on flavour without puffing up.
- **Why the boil.** A few seconds in boiling water set the outside before the oven does, which is where the shiny, chewy crust comes from.
- **Vital wheat gluten** is close to pure, dried gluten. It is there for chew.
