  - "½ cup water"

  - "2 sprigs thyme, leaves picked"
  - "1 clove garlic"
  - "½ small onion"
  - "3 tbsp olive oil, divided"
  - "2 medium green squash"
  - "2 medium yellow squash"
  - "2 medium Japanese eggplants"
  - "1 tsp kosher salt"
  - "1 tsp freshly ground black pepper"
  - "5 fresh parsley leaves, torn"

## Method

1. **Peel the tomatoes.** Cut a small X in the base of each tomato. Blanch them in boiling water for under a minute, just until the skin starts to split at the cuts, then move them to a bowl of ice water to cool.
2. **Char the peppers.** Set the peppers straight on a gas burner turned to high and turn them until they are black all over. Wrap them in foil for about 5 minutes to soften.
3. **Blend the sauce.** Peel the peppers and blend them smooth with some of the tomatoes, keeping the rest for slicing, plus the leaves of one rosemary sprig, the thyme, garlic, onion, stock, water and 1 tablespoon of the oil.
4. **Slice thin.** Heat the oven to 225°F. Slice the remaining tomatoes ⅛ inch thick with a sharp knife, and the squash and eggplants ⅛ inch thick on a mandoline.
5. **Shingle.** Spread a thin layer of the sauce in a shallow roasting pan. Overlap the slices around the edge in a repeating order of eggplant, tomato, yellow squash and green squash, each showing less than ¼ inch past the next. Fill the middle the same way, ring inside ring.
6. **Season.** Chop the leaves of the second rosemary sprig fine and scatter them over with 1 tablespoon of the oil, the salt and the pepper.
7. **Roast slowly.** Lay a sheet of parchment cut to the pan's size over the vegetables and roast for 90 minutes, taking the parchment off for the last 20. They are done when they are soft through but still hold their shape.
8. **Plate.** Stand a stack of slices upright inside a ring mold in the middle of a plate, fan a layer across the top and lift the mold away. Stir 1 tablespoon of sauce from the pan into the last of the oil, drizzle it around the stack and finish with the parsley.

## Notes

- **Source.** Adapted from Binging with Babish's [Confit Byaldi inspired by Ratatouille](https://www.babi.sh/recipes/ratatouille). The quantities, sizes, times and temperatures are from that page. The method is reworded step for step.
- **The film's dish.** Confit byaldi is a sliced take on ratatouille from the French chef Michel Guérard. Thomas Keller added the pepper sauce underneath and fanned the slices, and as a food consultant on Pixar's *Ratatouille* he chose this form for the dish the film's critic is served.
- **Even slices.** Slices of one thickness soften at the same rate, so nothing is mushy while its neighbour is still firm.
- **Covered, then not.** The parchment keeps the vegetables steaming in their own juice for most of the roast. The last 20 minutes without it let the top take a little colour.
