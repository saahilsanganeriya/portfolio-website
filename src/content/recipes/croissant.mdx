## Method

1. **Wake the yeast (day one, evening).** Whisk the yeast into the warm water and wait about 10 minutes, until foam gathers on top.
2. **Mix.** Whisk the flour, sugar and salt in a bowl. Stir in the yeast water, the yolk and the melted butter, then bring it together by hand.
3. **Slap and fold.** On a clean counter, lift the dough, slap it down and fold it over itself for 30 seconds to a minute, until the surface turns smooth. Ball it up, cover and chill for 10 minutes.
4. **Two turns.** Stretch one edge of the dough out and fold it across to the far side, then work around the whole edge the same way. Set it seam side down, cover and chill for 10 minutes. Turn it once more, then chill for 25 minutes.
5. **Square it, then rest overnight.** Fold a sheet of wax paper into a 7-inch (18 cm) square packet around the dough and roll until the dough fills it evenly. Chill for at least 12 hours.
6. **Make the butter block (day two).** Slice the butter about ½ inch (12 mm) thick, lay the slices in a 4-inch (10 cm) square on wax paper and fold the paper into a packet that size. Pound and roll until the butter fills it as one even, square sheet. Chill for 15 to 25 minutes.
7. **Enclose the butter.** Roll the dough's edges out so the square grows but keeps an even thickness. Set the butter on it as a diamond, stretch each corner of dough over it to the middle and pinch the seams shut. Turn it seam side down and give the butter a few minutes to soften slightly.
8. **The first fold, a book fold.** Press the rolling pin across the dough every inch or so, turning it as you go, to push the butter to the edges. Roll in one direction only to an 18-inch (46 cm) rectangle. Fold the bottom up three quarters of the way and the top down a quarter so the ends meet, then fold the whole thing in half. Tap the layers together, wrap and chill for 1 hour.
9. **The second fold, a letter fold.** Set the dough with its folded edges toward you, so this roll runs the other way, and roll it to 18 inches again. Fold the top third down and the bottom up over it. Wrap and chill for at least 1 hour, or up to 12.
10. **Cut.** Roll the dough to 10 inches (25 cm) wide and ¼ inch (6 mm) thick. Mark the bottom edge every 4½ inches (11.5 cm) and the top edge halfway between those marks. Join the marks with a long knife to cut tall triangles, 5 or 6 of them.
11. **Shape.** Stretch each triangle about an inch longer, unless it pulls back. Roll it up tightly from the wide end without pressing the layers flat, and set it on a parchment-lined tray with the tip underneath.
12. **Proof.** Brush with egg wash, cover with an upturned tray and proof for about 2 hours at 78°F (25°C), never above 80°F (27°C). An oven with only its light on and the door ajar works. They are ready when they have nearly doubled, the layers have begun to part and they wobble when the tray is nudged.
13. **Bake.** Brush again with egg wash. In a conventional oven, bake at 400°F (204°C) for 15 to 20 minutes, until golden and puffed. With convection, bake at 390°F (199°C) for 6 minutes, then at 330°F (165°C) for 10 to 15 more.

## Notes

- **Source.** Adapted from Joshua Weissman's [How To Make Proper Croissants Completely By Hand](https://www.joshuaweissman.com/recipes/best-croissant-recipe). The quantities, sizes, times and temperatures are his. The method is reworded step for step, and nothing else is taken from his page.
- **Why only two folds.** The book fold turns one sheet of butter into four layers, and the letter fold triples that to 12. Four letter folds would make 81 thinner ones.
- **When the dough pushes back.** If it springs back under the rolling pin, wrap it and chill it for about 30 minutes before trying again. Forcing it presses the butter into the dough, and the layers are lost.
- **Keep the proof cool.** Above 80°F (27°C) the butter starts to melt out of the layers before the oven can set them.
- **Timing.** The dough is mixed the evening before. On day two the butter block goes in during the morning, the folds take the late morning, the shaping happens around midday and the croissants bake about two hours later.
