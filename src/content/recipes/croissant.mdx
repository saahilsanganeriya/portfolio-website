## Method

1. **Start a sponge.** In a large bowl, mix the eggs and water with 1 tablespoon of the sugar, 362 g (3 cups) of the flour and the yeast until smooth. Leave it to bubble while you make the butter block.
2. **Make the butter block.** Cut the butter into 1-inch pieces. Beat it with the salt and flour on low speed with the paddle, only until smooth and free of lumps, and stop before it turns airy. Spread it into an 8-inch square on plastic wrap, wrap it and chill for 30 minutes.
3. **Finish the dough.** Stir the melted butter into the sponge. Whisk the rest of the sugar with 298 g (2½ cups) of the flour, the dry milk and the salt, add them, and mix until a dough forms. Knead for 5 minutes, and if it still sticks to a light touch, work in more flour 2 tablespoons at a time. Pat it into a 9-inch square, wrap and chill for 30 minutes.
4. **Lock in the butter.** Roll the dough to a 12-inch square. Set the butter block on it turned 45°, a diamond inside a square, and fold the four corners of dough in to meet over the middle. Wet the edges, pinch the seams shut, flour the top and turn the packet over.
5. **Fold twice.** Tap the packet all over with the rolling pin until it gives, then roll it to 20 by 10 inches, flouring lightly. Brush off the loose flour and fold it in thirds like a letter, with the edges squared up. Turn it so the open side faces you like a book, roll it to 20 by 10 again and fold it again. Wrap and chill for 30 minutes.
6. **Fold twice more, then rest.** Give the dough two more folds the same way. Wrap it and chill for at least 1 hour, or overnight. The dough can be frozen at this point.
7. **Cut.** Halve the dough and keep one half cold. Roll the other half to 13 by 18 inches and trim about ¼ inch from every edge. Cut it into six 4-by-9-inch rectangles, then cut each one corner to corner into two triangles. Stretch each triangle a little longer and cut a 1-inch notch in the middle of its short side.
8. **Shape.** Starting from the notched side, roll each triangle up toward its point, leading with the two corners of the notch, and finish with the point underneath. Set them on a parchment-lined sheet, bend the ends toward each other, cover and chill for 30 minutes.
9. **Proof.** Leave them covered at room temperature for 1 to 1½ hours. They are ready when they have grown noticeably and a gentle fingertip press leaves a dent that stays.
10. **Bake.** Near the end of the proof, heat the oven to 425°F. Beat the egg with the water and brush it over each croissant. Bake for 15 minutes, lower the oven to 350°F and bake 10 to 15 minutes more, until deep golden brown with no raw dough showing where the layers overlap. Cool on the pan for 20 minutes.

## Notes

- **Source.** Adapted from King Arthur Baking's [Baker's Croissants](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/bakers-croissants-recipe). The quantities, sizes, times and temperatures are King Arthur's. The method is reworded here, step for step, and the layout follows the way Binging with Babish and Joshua Weissman write recipes: ingredients grouped and weighed first, a numbered method, notes at the end.
- **Why the folds matter.** Each fold in thirds triples the layers. Four of them turn one sheet of butter into 81 thin ones, and the water in that butter turns to steam in the oven and pushes the layers apart.
- **Keep the butter and the dough equally firm.** Butter colder than the dough cracks into pieces when you roll it, and butter warmer than the dough smears into it. Either way the layers are lost. When the packet starts to feel soft, it goes back in the fridge.
- **Trim the edges.** The folded edges hold the dough back from rising, which is why step 7 cuts them off.
- **Bubbles and leaks.** Prick a trapped air bubble and press it flat. If butter breaks through the dough, dust the spot with flour and chill the dough as soon as that fold is done.
- **Planning.** The rested dough keeps overnight in the fridge, and the half you set aside in step 7 can go in the freezer for another day.
