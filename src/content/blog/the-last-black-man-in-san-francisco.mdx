Jimmie Fails plays a version of himself in Joe Talbot’s 2019 film: a young man who keeps going back to a Victorian house in San Francisco that he believes his grandfather built, and that his family no longer owns. He repaints the trim when the owners are out. Then the house falls empty.

It is one of the most interestingly shot films I have seen in a while, and it never looks like it is trying too hard. It looks breathtaking all the time. The usual compliment would be that every frame is a painting, and that line is too worn out to do this film justice.

Every shot and every scene gives you exactly what it needs to. The script, though, stays just off balance enough to keep the wonder of this world alive. Whenever I felt I was getting a firm grip on it, the editing humbled me.

It makes you feel a great deal without ever letting you say what it is you are feeling. I came out of it emotionally full, in the best way. Most of all, it is as gentle a film as I have ever seen.
