On 5 November 2018 I posted three pictures. The first was not mine. It was this one: a band of brown light across black, and about halfway down the band, a speck. The other two were. Rain on a car windscreen at dusk, with the street lamps and the brake lights ahead blurred into soft round dots.

The speck is Earth. My caption opened like a museum label: the date, 14 February 1990, the photographer, Voyager 1, and the distance, about six billion kilometres. Then a note on where to look, the bluish-white speck about halfway down the brown band on the right.

## What the picture is

Voyager 1 took it from about 40 times Earth's distance from the Sun, as one of 60 frames of a family portrait of the planets, shortly before its cameras were switched off. Earth covers 0.12 of a single pixel, and the brown bands are sunlight scattered by pointing the camera so close to the Sun. The picture exists because Carl Sagan wanted it, and in 1994 he named a book after it and wrote a passage that places everyone who ever lived on that speck.

## What I wrote under it

My caption did what Sagan's passage does, in fewer words. Everyone who ever existed, and everyone still to come, starts and ends on that dot. So do every loss and every achievement, all the pain and all the happiness.

The argument came near the end. Being that small, I wrote, makes people turn away, because turning away is easy. It is comfortable.

That was not a one-off. In March of that year I had posted the second law of thermodynamics, that the entropy of the universe only ever increases, and added that I thought this was beautiful. In October I posted a verse by Richard Feynman about being a universe of atoms, and an atom in the universe.

## Why it hangs in my room

The picture is on the wall of my room as a print, near a framed copy of the Voyager Golden Record, which has [a window of its own](/os#voyager).

My caption called the dot all that we are. It ended on two short sentences, and they are on a sticky note on my desk now: "I'm not afraid. I'm fascinated."
