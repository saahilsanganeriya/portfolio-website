When I was in kindergarten, my mother and I planted a mango seed in our garden in Delhi. Mango plants grow slowly. For years it seemed to me that the plant was keeping track of my life, growing when I grew and stalling when I stalled. When it died during a renovation of the house, I planted another. I could not let the idea of my mango tree go.

My father moved from Guwahati, in Assam, to Delhi and built his own spinning mill, Sanganeriya Spinning Mills. A spinning mill takes loose fiber and twists it into thread, the plain material that every piece of cloth begins as. The thread that runs through this site starts there.

I grew up reading a great deal and writing more than anyone asked me to. At fourteen I started a blog. It had begun the year before as a holiday assignment, a book review written week by week, and the idea of a blog of my own stayed with me. On my own blog I wrote stories, opinions, a short poem about chaos and a long review of *La La Land*. Its about page called me a dreamer who needed to do something that made a difference. At sixteen I started a second blog, about physics. The same year I kept a photography blog with the tagline “A Narrative in a Frame.” The story reel on this site borrows the name.

At eighteen I moved to the United States to study computer science at Georgia Tech, in Atlanta. My father moved cities to build his mill. I moved continents to learn. Leaving was not easy, and it is still hard to talk about.

I finished a bachelor’s degree in 2024 and a master’s in computer science, focused on machine learning, in 2025. Along the way I helped build [SAMCell](/projects/samcell), a model that finds and outlines individual cells in microscope images without anyone having to point at them; the paper was published in *PLOS ONE* in 2025. I worked as a data engineer at The Coca-Cola Company and then at Amazon, where I built a [real-time streaming pipeline](/projects/real-time-streaming).

Now I am a software engineer at Mercor, in San Francisco, on a team that prepares anonymized company data for training AI models. I build entity resolution, which works out which names, emails and handles belong to the same real person, and the system that gives each of those people one consistent fake identity. The [projects](/projects) page has the details, and the [writing](/blog) is where I think out loud.

Home is the part I have not settled. As a teenager I wrote a poem that ended by calling chaos home, and the chaos in it smelled like a Delhi market on a summer afternoon. Delhi is a long way from here. The question of India or America has never really gone away. There is a comforting idea that home is everywhere, because the sky is the same everywhere. I understand the appeal and only half believe it.

What I am sure of is the other part. I left home to do work that matters, and I want it to be big enough to have been worth the distance. The fourteen-year-old with the blog wanted the same thing.

There is a mango plant on the windowsill of the room on this site’s front page. It is a slow one.
