When I was in kindergarten, my mother and I planted a mango seed in our garden in Delhi. Mango plants grow slowly, and for years I was convinced mine was keeping pace with me. It grew when I grew. When I stalled, it stalled too. It died during a renovation of the house, and I planted another, because I could not let go of the idea of my own mango tree. Birds built a nest in the new one. I checked on the eggs every day for a week, until one day there were only shells.

My father came to Delhi from Guwahati, in Assam, and built a spinning mill of his own, Sanganeriya Spinning Mills. A spinning mill takes loose fibre and twists it into thread, the plain stuff every piece of cloth starts as. He moved to a new city and made something there that carries our name. The thread that runs through this site starts in his mill.

<MePhoto file="schoolboy-car" side="left" />

I was a bookish kid who wrote more than anyone asked me to. At fourteen I started a blog, and its about page called me a dreamer who had to do something that made a difference. Over the next few years I wrote a poem about chaos on it, and a long review of *La La Land*.

At eighteen I got on a flight to Atlanta to study computer science at Georgia Tech. My father had moved cities to build his mill. I moved continents. Leaving was not easy, and it is still hard to talk about.

<MePhoto file="convertible-sky" side="right" />

I stayed on for a master’s, then moved west, and now I live in San Francisco and write software at Mercor.

Home is the part I have not settled. The chaos poem, written at sixteen, ended by calling chaos home, and the chaos in it smelled like a Delhi market on a late summer afternoon. India or America is a question that has never quite gone away. There is a comforting idea that home is everywhere, because the sky is the same everywhere. I like it, and I only half believe it. The window in the room on this site’s front page looks out on San Francisco on one side and Delhi on the other, each lit by its own clock. That is the closest I have come to an answer.

<MePhoto file="prague" side="left" />

What I am sure of is the other part. I came a long way to do this work, and I want it to be big enough to have been worth the distance. The fourteen-year-old with the blog wanted the same thing.

I love movies about making greatness happen. The two films I have logged most on Letterboxd are *La La Land* and *Babylon*, five times each so far. Both are about people who want something enormous and pay for it.

I cook up code and I cook. I bake, too. The little oven in the room on the front page holds whatever I am building right now. What came out of it is on the [projects](/projects) page, and the [writing](/blog) is where I think out loud.

There is a mango plant on the windowsill in that room, below the two cities. It is a slow one.
