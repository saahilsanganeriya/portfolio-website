A phase-contrast image of kidney cells looks like grey cobblestones pressed together. A biologist who
wants to count them, or measure each one, needs an outline around every cell, and one image can hold
about 300. Segment Anything, Meta's general segmentation model, will outline almost anything you click
on. Clicking 300 times per image is the problem.

## What it does

SAMCell fine-tunes Segment Anything so it needs no clicks at all. Instead of masks, it predicts a distance
map: for every pixel, how far it sits from the nearest cell border. Two thresholds turn that map into
cells. A high one leaves one seed inside each cell, a low one marks every pixel that belongs to some cell,
and a watershed grows each seed until it meets its neighbours. That last step is what pulls apart cells
that touch.

## My part

I proposed the distance-map regression with watershed post-processing, and I trained the model. We
fine-tuned the image encoder and mask decoder and froze the prompt encoder, which has nothing left to do.
Training used 5,000+ LIVECell images in PyTorch, with CLAHE contrast enhancement and inference over
256×256 sliding windows.

The hard part was generalisation. Every LIVECell image comes from the same microscope, so a model can ace
the test set and fall apart on someone else's bench. I built two new datasets for that question, PBL-HEK
and PBL-N2a: phase-contrast images of two common cell lines, taken on different microscopes, 5 images
each with about 300 cells per image. We used them only for zero-shot testing.

## Results

On LIVECell, SAMCell scored 0.77 OP-CSB against 0.68 for Cellpose, the strongest baseline. On PBL-HEK, a
microscope and cell line it had never seen, it nearly doubled Cellpose: 0.60 against 0.32.

A model only helps biologists if they can run it. I shipped [samcell-napari](https://github.com/saahilsanganeriya/samcell-napari), a plugin for the napari image
viewer: point it at a model, set the two thresholds, and the cells come back as a labels layer. It is on
PyPI, and `pip install "napari[all]" samcell-napari` installs it with napari. The public code lives in my fork of the first author's repository, where 17 of the 22 commits are
mine.

The paper is VandeLoo AD, Malta NJ (joint first authors), Sanganeriya S, Aponte E, van Zyl C, Xu D,
Forest C, *SAMCell: Generalized label-free biological cell segmentation with segment anything*, PLOS ONE,
2025.

## What I would change

The two new datasets are small, 10 images in all. That is enough to show the zero-shot gap, not enough to
rank two close methods. Every method also scored much higher on PBL-N2a, whose round, high-contrast cells
are easier, so a single average would hide where the real difficulty is.

[DINOCell](https://github.com/saahilsanganeriya/DINOCell) is my follow-up: the same distance-map head on a DINOv3 self-supervised backbone, pretrained on
Cell Painting images streamed from a public bucket. It has no results yet. I list it because it is where
the idea goes next, not because it works.
