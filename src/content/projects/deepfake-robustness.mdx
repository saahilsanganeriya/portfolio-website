We pointed a published fake-image detector at about 19,000 modern real and AI-generated images. It caught 3.8% of the fakes.

Its overall accuracy was 51%, a coin flip. The detector came from a well-known paper, “CNN-generated images are surprisingly easy to spot… for now”: a ResNet-50 trained on GAN images, with random blur and JPEG compression added so it would survive light editing. Modern generators, and the way platforms recompress, sharpen and filter what people post, had moved past it.

## Edits a platform might make

For a machine-learning security course, three of us fine-tuned that detector on a modern dataset, once on clean images and once per kind of edit.

   colour jitter | sharpening x2 | WebP, not JPEG

                    v

<figcaption>The experiment: the same detector fine-tuned on images edited the way social media edits them, one kind of edit at a time.</figcaption>
</figure>

| Detector | Accuracy | Fakes caught |
|---|---:|---:|
| Published, as is | 51% | 3.8% |
| Fine-tuned on clean images | 71–77% | 98–99% |
| Fine-tuned on colour-jittered images | 73.7% | 98.1% |
| Fine-tuned on sharpened images | 82.6% | 99.2% |
| Fine-tuned with WebP compression | 82.9% | 93.6% |

Most of the jump came from fine-tuning on modern images at all. The edits added about 3 to 10 points on top, depending on the edit. Sharpening, my experiment, helped the real-image side most, perhaps because it brings out genuine detail in real photos as well as artifacts in fakes. That is our guess, and the paper does not prove it.

## What the 83% gives up

The 83% on my résumé is the WebP model, and it catches fewer fakes than the sharpened one, 93.6% against 99.2%, at almost the same accuracy. If a missed fake is the expensive mistake, the sharpened model is the one to ship.

## What I would change

We fine-tuned and tested on splits of one dataset. So these numbers say the detector adapts to this kind of image, not that it would catch a generator it has never seen, which is the case that matters. I would hold out a whole generator first.

One thing in our own tables bothers me now. In the colour and sharpening experiments, every model scored exactly the same on the edited test images as on the untouched ones, to a tenth of a percent. Edits that leave a score untouched are possible, but identical scores for every model deserved a second look before we leaned on them.
