A detector that catches AI-generated images in a lab can fail the moment someone posts the image.
Platforms recompress it, people sharpen it and add filters, and the faint traces a detector relies on get
smoothed away.

We started from a published universal detector, a ResNet-50 trained on GAN images. On a modern set of
about 19,000 real and AI-generated images it reached 51% accuracy, a coin flip, and caught 3.8% of the
fakes.

We fine-tuned it on the same kind of images with social-media edits applied: WebP compression in place of
the JPEG compression from its original training, colour jitter, and sharpening. The best version reached
83% accuracy while still catching over 93% of the fakes. Sharpening helped the real-image side the most,
perhaps because it brings out genuine detail in real photos as well as artifacts in fakes. That is our
guess, and the paper does not prove it.

## What I would change

We fine-tuned and tested on splits of one dataset. So 83% says the detector adapts to this kind of image.
It does not say the detector would catch images from a generator it has never seen, which is the case
that matters. I would test on a held-out generator first.

The code lives in a teammate's fork of the original detector, and its main branch is their work and the
original authors'. Mine is one commit, on the unmerged
[`sharpening` branch](https://github.com/saahilsanganeriya/DeepFakeDetection/tree/sharpening) of my copy:
the paper's sharpening experiment. The write-up was a course paper and is not online.
