In our Frogger, the river is not dangerous. A turtle can walk straight across it.

Five of us built this game for CS 2340 at Georgia Tech in spring 2023, over five sprints. It is an Android app in plain Java, with no game engine, and the four Ninja Turtles are the characters you can play. Frogger is a good first game because the rules fit in one sentence: cross the road, then the river, and do not get hit or fall in.

You pick a name, a difficulty and a turtle. Easy gives you 5 lives, medium 3 and hard 1. Five road lanes carry 12 vehicles at three speeds, and the river has 4 lanes of floating logs. Each step further up the screen scores a point, and steps in the river score double. A hit costs a life and resets your score.

## Two timers, one activity

Everything runs in one activity, on two timers.

<figure class="os-figure">

```text
 timer 1, every 10 ms:
   arrow held?  move the turtle 4 pixels

 timer 2, every 6 ms, one task per vehicle and log:
   move it by its lane speed, wrap at the edge
   vehicle?  turtle inside its box?  lose a life
   log?      (water and log checks commented out)
```

<figcaption>The game's two timers: vehicles check the turtle on every tick, but the river's checks never run, so nothing in the river can end a life.</figcaption>
</figure>

It was quick to build to a sprint deadline. All of it, from set-up to the win and lose screens, sits in a 559-line `MainActivity.java`, and the lanes are fixed pixel positions that do not scale to the screen.

The repository records authors only for the tests. I wrote 8 of the 40, and I put the finished app on GitHub after the term.

## What the tests missed

There are 40 tests and not one assertion. They mock the activity and check nothing, so they could not have caught the river. The water check is written, and it is never called.

## What I would change

The game state, the drawing and the loop belong in separate pieces that can be tested on their own, and the river needs a test that walks a turtle into the water and expects it to die. The lanes should be placed relative to the screen. The turtle artwork belongs to its owners, so none of it appears here.
