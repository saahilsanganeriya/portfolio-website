Every skill our agent learned did the same thing. A few epochs in, it pressed the same button whatever skill we asked for.

METRA teaches an agent a set of distinct skills with no reward at all. Each skill should carry the agent somewhere different, measured in a learned representation of the states it visits. The original paper worked on continuous control, robots with joints. For a deep reinforcement learning course, six of us wanted to know whether it carried over to Ms. Pac-Man: 9 joystick actions and raw 210 by 160 pixel frames.

## Rebuilding it

The original code was hard to adapt, so the team rewrote METRA from scratch and swapped its policy learner for SAC-Discrete, the version of soft actor-critic built for a fixed set of actions. I worked on that adaptation and on finding out why it failed.

```text
                            |
 skill z  ---------------> reward: how far that step
 (fixed for the episode)    went in z's direction

 step ~ 3e-8 to 6e-8  ->  reward ~ 0  ->  nothing to learn
```

## Why the skills collapsed

The representation barely moved from one frame to the next. In the team’s logged runs, the mean step between consecutive states was 2.9e-8 to 6.4e-8. With almost no change to measure, the reward was almost zero, and with a fixed temperature the policy’s randomness fell to nothing and it settled on one action. t-SNE plots of the representation showed no structure, and silhouette scores against the skill labels stayed at or below 0.2.

The dead ends are results too. Scaling the reward up did nothing. A warm-up that trained the encoder alone collapsed again as soon as policy training resumed. Widening the skill space from 4 to 24 dimensions, and switching to continuous skills, did not help.

A decaying temperature helped early exploration and fixed nothing underneath. METRA’s own limit on how far the representation may move was never reached, so it was not the cause.

What mattered was the encoder. A pretrained ResNet-18 raised the step to between 1.1e-3 and 1.0e-2, about five orders of magnitude. Even then the skills did not separate: silhouette scores stayed between -0.10 and 0.05.

## What I would change

The representation was the bottleneck all along, and we tuned around it first. I would measure the representation before anything else, and tune the policy only once it moves.

The team’s code and the training logs these numbers come from are in a [shared repository](https://github.com/saahilsanganeriya/Scratch-Implementation). Every code commit there is a teammate’s. The report was a course paper and is not online.
