METRA teaches an agent a set of distinct skills with no reward at all. Each skill should carry the agent
somewhere different, measured in a learned representation of the states it visits. The original paper
worked on continuous control, robots with joints. We wanted to know whether it carried over to a game
played with a joystick: discrete actions and raw pixels.

The original code was hard to adapt, so the team rewrote METRA from scratch and swapped its policy learner
for SAC-Discrete, the version of soft actor-critic built for a fixed set of actions. The test bed was Ms.
Pac-Man.

It mostly did not work, and the reason was the interesting part. The skills collapsed into the same
behaviour. t-SNE plots of the representation showed no structure, and silhouette scores against the skill
labels stayed at or below 0.2. The representation barely moved from one frame to the next: the step
between consecutive states was around 1e-9. With almost no change to measure, the intrinsic reward was
almost zero, and the policy fell back to choosing one action.

A warm-up phase that trained the encoder alone collapsed again as soon as policy training resumed. A
deeper pixel encoder, a ResNet-18, moved the step from about 1e-9 to about 1e-3. That was the change that
mattered, though even then the skills did not separate cleanly.

## What I would change

We also widened the skill space from 4 to 24 dimensions and tried continuous skills. Neither helped,
because the representation was the bottleneck all along. I would measure the representation first and
tune everything around it second.
