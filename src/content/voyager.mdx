## What it is

The Golden Record is a 12-inch phonograph record made of gold-plated copper. NASA launched one on each of its two Voyager spacecraft in 1977. It was made to tell the story of our world to anyone out there who finds it.

Voyager 1 left Earth on 5 September 1977. In August 2012 it became the first spacecraft to leave the heliosphere, the bubble the Sun's wind blows around the planets. In August 2024 NASA put it 164.7 times as far from Earth as the Sun is.

## What is on it

A committee chaired by Carl Sagan, of Cornell University, chose what went on the record.

- 115 images, stored in analog form.
- Spoken greetings in 55 languages, from Akkadian, spoken in Sumer about six thousand years ago, to Wu, a modern Chinese dialect.
- Sounds of Earth: surf, wind and thunder, birds, whales and other animals.
- A 90-minute selection of music from many cultures and eras.
- Printed messages from President Jimmy Carter and the UN Secretary-General, Kurt Waldheim.

The sound is meant to be played at 16⅔ revolutions per minute.

## How to play it

The cover carries the instructions, etched into it: how to play the record, how to find Earth, and a way to tell how long the record has been in space. The drawings show it played from the outside in, and give one turn of the record as 3.6 seconds, written in binary.

## Why it is on my wall

It hangs near a print of the Pale Blue Dot, the picture of Earth that Voyager 1 took in February 1990 from about six billion kilometres away. The record and the picture went out on the same spacecraft, and I wrote about the picture in [A pale blue dot](/blog/pale-blue-dot).
